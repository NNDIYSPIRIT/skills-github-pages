If you looked at my workbench a while ago, you’d find a mess of jumper wires, breadboards, 18650 Li-ion batteries, and microcontrollers scattered everywhere. Fast forward to today, and you'll find me knee-deep in C++, Python, version control, and algorithms as a first-year Computer Science Engineering student.

People often ask me: *How did you go from building Wi-Fi robot cars with ESP8266 modules to studying Computer Science?* 

The truth is, they were never two separate paths. They are two halves of the exact same puzzle.

## ⚡ Sparking the Maker Flame

My journey into tech started with physical components. Building DIY electronics—like an ESP8266-powered robot car. 

There is a unique thrill in writing code, flashing it to a chip, and watching real motors spin. As I dive into project research and look ahead to building more complex systems, I keep hitting a recurring realization: **the physical hardware is only as smart as the software driving it.**

## 🧠 Why Computer Science?

Choosing to major in Computer Science Engineering felt like the natural evolution of being a maker. 

* **Bridging the Gap:** Whether you're optimizing firmware for low-power IoT microcontrollers or structuring full-stack web applications, logic is universal.
* **The Power of Scale:** Hardware is limited by physical components; software is limited only by architecture and imagination. 
* **The Developer Mindset:** Transitioning from high school Bio-Maths into engineering taught me to approach problems methodically—breaking down massive challenges into manageable modules and clean Git commits.

Today, **NN DIT SPIRIT** represents that exact intersection of hardware and software. Whether it's experimenting with automation, building tools, or pushing new robotics projects, I'm just getting started.

*Stay curious, keep building.*
